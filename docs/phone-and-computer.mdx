---
title: "Phone and computer"
description: "Pair CassetteCat for Android with CassetteCat Desktop to control playback, move music between them and keep your library in sync."
---

Pair the Android app with CassetteCat Desktop on the same Wi-Fi and they work as one. Your phone becomes a remote for the computer, what you're playing moves between them with its queue, and your likes, playlists and Listening Record stay in sync. The phone also backs itself up to the computer every day.

Both apps work fine on their own, so pairing is optional. Everything stays on your own network, encrypted, with no account and no internet connection involved.

You need CassetteCat for Android 1.8.0 or later and CassetteCat Desktop 0.9.0 or later.

## Pair your phone

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on the phone remote on the computer">
    In CassetteCat Desktop, check that **Settings > Phone Remote > Control From Your Phone** is on. It's on by default.

    The first time it's on, Windows may ask whether CassetteCat can use your network. Allow it on private networks, or your phone won't find the computer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the computer on your phone">
    On the phone, open **Settings > Library & Hardware > Desktop Remote**, or tap **Devices** in Now Playing and then **Set up a computer**. Your computer appears under **Computers on this Wi-Fi**. Tap it. If it isn't there, tap **Search again**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Allow the phone on the computer">
    The computer asks **Connect *your phone*?** Click **Allow**. The phone shows **Paired**, and the computer lists it under **Paired Phones**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Pairing is remembered, even when the computer gets a new address on your network.

<Warning>
Only allow phones you own. A paired phone can control playback, sync your library data and back up to that computer.
</Warning>

### Pair by address instead

When the phone can't find the computer, it's usually a guest network or a router that keeps devices apart. You can type the address in instead:

1. On the computer, open **Settings > Phone Remote > Pair Manually** and click **Copy**. You get something like `192.168.1.20:47800#ABC234`: the computer's address, then `#`, then the pairing code.
2. On the phone, enter the whole thing in **Desktop Remote**, including the part after `#`.

**New Code** on the computer makes a fresh pairing code. Phones paired by address have to pair again; phones you approved with **Allow** stay paired.

## Control the computer from your phone

In **Desktop Remote**, tap your computer. The row changes to **Controlling from this phone**, and Now Playing on the phone shows and controls what the computer plays: play, pause, skip, seek, volume, shuffle, repeat and the queue. The phone's notification, lock screen and volume keys work too.

While a phone is in control, the computer shows a phone icon in its player bar saying **Controlled from *your phone***.

Tap the row again to stop controlling it. The computer keeps playing.

## Play separately on each

To have the phone and the computer each play their own music, tap **Disconnect** in the phone's **Devices** sheet or in **Desktop Remote**, or click **Disconnect** in the computer's phone remote window. They stay paired, and likes, playlists, listens and backups keep syncing, but neither one controls or pauses the other. Pick the computer in **Devices** again to reconnect.

## Move music between phone and computer

Music plays on one device at a time, and the song, queue and position move with it.

- **Phone to computer**: tap **Devices** in Now Playing and choose your computer. The phone stops. The computer also appears in Android's own output switcher, in the quick settings media controls.
- **Computer to phone**: on the computer, open the phone remote from the phone button in the player bar and click **Continue on *your phone***. The phone picks up the same song at the same position.
- **Take over what the phone plays**: when the phone is playing and the computer isn't, the computer's player bar shows **Playing on *your phone*** with play, pause and next buttons. **Play Here** moves the music to the computer.

To queue a single song on the other device, use **Play Next on Computer** in a song's options on the phone, or **Play Next on Phone** in a song's menu on the computer.

Songs are matched by title and artist, so a song only moves if both libraries have it.

## Play your computer's music on the phone

Open the computer's library from **Library** on the phone (the computer button at the top), or **Desktop Remote > Browse your computer's music** in Settings.

- **Search** by song, artist or album.
- **Tap a song** to play it where you're listening: on the computer while you're controlling it, otherwise streamed to the phone over Wi-Fi.
- **The three dots** beside a song choose the other one: **Play on This Phone** or **Play on Computer**.

Streaming needs the computer on, with CassetteCat Desktop open, on the same Wi-Fi. Songs streamed this way aren't saved on the phone.

## What stays in sync

- **Likes** sync both ways, for songs both libraries have.
- **Playlists**: on the phone, open a playlist's options and choose **Send to Computer**. A playlist with the same name on the computer is replaced, and you're told how many songs matched. To copy one the other way, tap **Get a playlist from your computer** in **Library > Playlists**.
- **Listening Record**: plays on either device count in one history and rank the same way. The **Sync** button on the computer's Listening Record page brings them together straight away.

## Back up to your computer

The phone backs itself up to the paired computer once a day when you open the app. The backup includes your playlists, favourites, listening statistics, folder filters and settings. Server passwords are left out; see [Backup, restore and privacy](/android/backup-and-privacy).

On the phone, **Settings > Backup & Restore** has two buttons under **Computer**:

- **Back Up to Computer** makes a backup now.
- **Restore from Computer** replaces the phone's data with the latest backup on the computer. Use it after resetting or replacing your phone.

The computer keeps the previous backup alongside the latest one.

## Privacy and security

Everything stays on your network. The connection is encrypted: the computer makes its own certificate the first time, and the phone remembers it when you pair. If the computer's certificate ever changes, for example after a reinstall that removed its data, the phone asks you to pair again.

## Stop using it

To keep the pairing but play separately, see [Play separately on each](#play-separately-on-each). To stop for good:

- **On the phone**: **Desktop Remote > Forget this computer**.
- **On the computer**: click **Remove** next to the phone under **Paired Phones**. To stop phones connecting at all, turn off **Control From Your Phone**.

## If something goes wrong

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="The phone can't find the computer">

- Both devices must be on the same Wi-Fi network. Guest networks and some office, hotel or router setups stop devices seeing each other; use your main network.
- On the computer, CassetteCat Desktop must be open, **Settings > Phone Remote > Control From Your Phone** on, and **Offline Blackout Mode** off.
- On Windows, allow CassetteCat through the firewall on private networks. If you said no to the prompt earlier, allow it in **Windows Security > Firewall & network protection > Allow an app through firewall**.
- CassetteCat uses port 47800 for the connection (TCP) and for being found (UDP). If you run your own firewall, allow both on your local network.
- If it still isn't found, [pair by address](#pair-by-address-instead).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The phone says the code doesn't match">

Copy the address again from the computer and enter all of it, including the part after `#`. After ten wrong codes in a row the computer refuses all requests for one minute, so wait a minute before trying again.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="The phone asks to pair again">

That happens when someone clicked **New Code** on the computer, when the phone was removed under **Paired Phones**, or when the computer's certificate changed, for example after its data was deleted. Pair again as above.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="A song didn't move to the other device">

Songs are matched by title and artist. If the other library has different tags for the song, or doesn't have it, it can't move. Make the title and artist match.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Backups to the computer fail">

The computer must be on, with CassetteCat Desktop open, on the same Wi-Fi. The phone tries again the next day, or tap **Back Up to Computer** in **Settings > Backup & Restore**.

</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
